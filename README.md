## About Me

I am a graduate student in the Master of Information Systems Management (MISM) program at Carnegie Mellon University, where I work at the intersection of data, software engineering, and artificial intelligence. Before Carnegie Mellon, I earned a Bachelor of Technology in Electronics Engineering from the University of Mumbai, and I have spent the years since turning messy, real-world data into systems and decisions that people can act on. As an Associate Consultant at EY, I built backend services and cloud infrastructure, automated document processing with OCR, and developed predictive sales forecasting that helped teams see what was coming rather than only what had already happened. More recently, as a Software Engineer Intern at ServiceLink, I designed enterprise validation rules and built Streamlit analytics dashboards that tracked more than twenty key performance indicators for business stakeholders.

What connects these experiences is a steady interest in communication: a model or a metric only matters once someone understands it well enough to make a decision. I am drawn to data analytics and visualization, machine learning and generative AI, and the emerging space of agentic, intelligent automation, and I enjoy the craft of making technical results legible to the people who depend on them. That is exactly why this course appeals to me. I want to get better at the last and hardest step of analytical work: shaping data into a clear, honest story that an audience can follow and trust.

## What I Hope to Learn

My goal in this course is to become fluent in the visual language of data. I want to create clear and meaningful visualizations, choose the right chart for a given dataset and question, and use color, typography, and visual hierarchy with intention rather than by default. Just as importantly, I want to sharpen my eye for visualizations that mislead or confuse, so I can recognize those patterns in the work of others and avoid them in my own. Having built dashboards and forecasting tools professionally, I know how easy it is to produce a chart that is technically correct but still leaves its audience lost, and I want to close that gap.

I am especially interested in learning to communicate insights to both technical and nontechnical audiences, and in applying data storytelling to the domains I care about most: business intelligence, AI, and data-driven decision-making. The ability to translate an analytical result into a narrative that a product team, an executive, or a non-specialist can act on feels central to the kind of work I want to do in software engineering, analytics, and AI. I expect the habits I build here to carry directly into how I design dashboards, present model results, and frame the tradeoffs behind automated systems.
